# Shader Breakdown 16 — Hexagonal (Honeycomb) Grid

Hexagonal patterns appear in honeycombs, cellular structures, and "pixel-art on a hex grid." A hex grid is a **triangular/rhombic lattice with six-fold symmetry**, and it is built by a *skewed* repetition rather than a rectangular one. This breakdown derives the hex-grid coordinate transform used by procedural shaders.

float honeycomb(vec2 p, float d){
    vec2 q = hexCoords(p);

## 2. Mathematics

### 2.1 The triangular lattice and axial coordinates

A **hex lattice** is, in `axial` coordinates, the set of points generated by two basis vectors separated by $60^\circ$ (i.e. the generators of the triangular lattice):
$$
\mathbf a_1=(1,0),\qquad \mathbf a_2=\Big(\frac12,\ \frac{\sqrt3}{2}\Big).
$$
The lattice is
$$
\mathbf L=\{m\mathbf a_1+n\mathbf a_2:\ m,n\in\mathbb Z\}.
$$
The **inverse transform** maps a point $\mathbf p$ to its hex-cell axial coordinates. For a pointy-top hex grid aligned so that $\mathbf a_1=(1,0)$ and $\mathbf a_2=(1/2,\sqrt3/2)$, the fractional axial coordinate of $\mathbf p$ is obtained by solving
$$
\mathbf p=\xi\,\mathbf a_1+\eta\,\mathbf a_2,
$$
i.e.
$$
[\xi,\eta]=\mathbf p\,\begin{pmatrix}1 & 0\\ \tfrac12 & \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}\end{pmatrix}^{-1}
=\Big(p_x-\tfrac{p_y}{\sqrt3},\ \tfrac{2p_y}{\sqrt3}\Big).
$$

**The skew.** The key is that the hex lattice is the *square lattice sheared* by the ratio $1/\sqrt3$. In the shader, `h = vec2(1.0, 1/sqrt(3))` and `p*h` is the skew that turns the square lattice into the hex lattice. Both `floor` and `fract` of the skewed coordinate give the cell index and the local position in the cell.

**Why hex, not square.** A *square* lattice partitioning gives axis-aligned "blocks." A hex lattice (triangular lattice of cell centers) gives six-fold-symmetric rows of cells. The difference is purely the shear $1/\sqrt3$ and the two-step row offset.

### 2.2 The cell-centre correction

Because the hex grid is offset (each odd row is shifted by half a cell), a naive `floor(p*h)` gives a *rhombic* partition, which is not the hex one. The standard correction picks, for each point, the hex cell whose center is closest — using the **axial** coordinate and a small branch. The result is the "pointy-top" or "flat-top" honeycomb.

### 2.3 The honeycomb field

The honeycomb `honeycomb(p,d)` computes `abs(d - length(fract(q)-0.5))`. This is the distance from $\mathbf p$ to the set of hexagon *centers* at spacing $d$, taken with an `abs` so the borders (where the distance equals $d$... actually where `length(fract(q)-0.5) == d`) are the cell walls. The result is a network of hexagonal walls — a honeycomb.

**Field class.** This is a distance to a hexagonal array of points, then thresholded. It is 1-Lipschitz (distance to points) up to the fold; the `abs` creates a "wall" at a fixed distance around each center, giving the honeycomb network. It is a valid distance bound.

## 3. Where hex grids are used

- **Flat-shaded low-poly / honeycomb motifs.**
- **Voronoi on a hex seed**, for organic cells (the hexagonal arrangement plus a jitter).
- **"Hexagonal crystal"** patterns in materials.
- **Grid-based ray marching** (iq's hexgrid traversal) — marching a ray through a hex cell lattice with analytic AO (Breakdown 31).

## 4. The "thinking process"

1. **Ideas.** "I want honeycomb / hexagonal cells." → a triangular lattice.
2. **Coordinates.** "Use basis vectors at 60°." → shear by $1/\sqrt3$ (`h = vec2(1, 1/sqrt3)`).
3. **Cells.** "Pick the nearest cell center." → axial `floor`/`fract` with a row correction.
4. **Walls.** "Draw the borders." → `abs(d - length(fract-0.5))`.

## 5. Extensions

- **Flat-top vs. pointy-top.** Rotate the basis by $30^\circ$ (or swap $\mathbf a_1,\mathbf a_2$).
- **Voronoi-hex.** Jitter each hex center with a hash; the honeycomb becomes an organic Voronoi (Chapter 18).
- **Beveled/wall height.** Use the distance-to-centre as a "height" to displace the wall, giving a 3D honeycomb.
- **Hex SDF.** For a 3D honeycomb surface, take the 2D hex distance and extrude it (Chapter 8.13), producing a prismatic honeycomb.
